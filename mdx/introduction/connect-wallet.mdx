# Connecting to the app 🔑

Your account is anchored to an Ethereum-compatible address. That address is used
to open your workspace, sign in to the web app, sign governance actions, publish
workspace records, and, when needed, authorize encrypted storage access.

## Choosing a connection method

You can sign in in a self-sovereign, privacy-preserving manner by clicking "Connect Wallet" on the top right corner of the screen. There, you can either:

- Sign in with passkeys. This option creates an Ethereum-compatible wallet from a
  passkey credential and stores an encrypted backup on Organigram servers. It is the
  easiest and recommended option for most users on compatible browsers.

<LoginMethodButton sx={{ mb: 5 }} method='passkey' />

- If your organization uses Google Workspace, you can verify that organization
  identity from Account Settings after signing in. Google confirms your email
  domain, but your wallet address remains the Organigram account used to sign in
  and sign actions.

<LoginMethodButton sx={{ mb: 5 }} method='google' />

- Use a crypto wallet such as Rabby, MetaMask, Rainbow, Trust Wallet, Ledger, or
  any supported wallet available through
  [WalletConnect](https://walletguide.walletconnect.network/).

<LoginMethodButton sx={{ mb: 5 }} method='wallet' />

- Sign in with an email code. This option verifies that you control the email
  address, then reuses the wallet address already linked to that email. If no
  wallet is linked to that email, Organigram helps you create the wallet with a passkey.

<LoginMethodButton sx={{ mb: 5 }} method='email' />

## Compliance and non-custody

Whichever connection option, your private keys and assets are never shared with
the app in clear text. With passkeys, Organigram stores encrypted vault envelopes
only; Google and email can recover an Organigram session, but they cannot decrypt
or sign with your wallet.

You can also verify your identity with an [ID
certificate](/docs/certified/id-certificates) to get access to [advanced
signatures](/docs/certified/advanced-signatures) and [legally-binding
actions](/docs/certified/binding-actions). In that case, we recommend using new addresses that have never been used on a public network before, such as with passkey-generated wallets.

For the full list of the information we access and how we use it in these services, you can refer to our [privacy policy](/docs/reference/privacy).
